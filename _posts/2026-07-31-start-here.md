## Background (On Me)
> (<i>Before you look at my em-dashes and squint about this being written by an LLM, you'll have to pry the em-dash out of my cold, dead, academically-inclined hands.)</i>

Over a decade ago (circa 2013), I became fascinated with immunology - more specifically - adaptive immunology. Every second of every day (in your typical person), thousands of receptors are being created at a genetic level in a somewhat stochastic way, to try to "fact check" or identify "self versus non-self" (Soon there will be a link to an article about this very topic because this is being further refined as a concept). A remarkable system that has been refined over millions of years. <i>(It also helped that the small startup I was employed by was literally based around its study, but I still hold a fascination with it to this day.)</i>

---

At this point in my career, I am in the government contracting industry with a focus on data science, artificial intelligence, and machine learning - among other things.

In the last few years, I have been exposed to the concept of graph databases and the mathematics related to graphs in general and, personally, I think the entire field of bioinformatics could benefit from it. If you look at the glorious database(s) of the [National Institute of Biotechnology - NCBI](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), "everything the light touches" is a <b>tabular</b> reference.

The reason I bring this up is that, during the SARS-CoV-2 (COVID, COVID-19) pandemic - and despite vaccination - I still ended up testing positive for the virus ***six*** different times between 2021 and 2023. Here, in the year 2026, I am affected by a wide-array of symptoms - most of which seem to be immunological, cardiovascular, and pulmonary in origin. Rather than blindly trust a physician knew the latest and greatest of research related to "long COVID", I wanted to try to study and remediate some of my concerns. Experiment with/on myself, as it were.

This self-experimentation (mostly supplementation and diet) came with long periods of trying to sift through [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) searches, attempts to carefully use search terms to avoid confirmation bias, and trying to wrangle **so. much. fucking. research.** was a mind-boggling task (even when knows what they are doing or what they are looking for).

Rather than focus on a single condition or my own personal anecdotes, I wanted to try to create a **multi-functional** and **interdisciplinary** tool that could be used for topics.
